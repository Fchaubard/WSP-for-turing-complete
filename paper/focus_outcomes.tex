The 60 WSP requests returned executable arrays for all 174 method--setting combinations. Against Adam followed by BFGS, examples-only WSP returned lower training loss in 79 of 87 settings and higher loss in eight. With an absolute tolerance of $10^{-10}$ on the difference, 33 settings were ties, 48 favored WSP and six favored the gradient fit. On the outer test, examples-only WSP had lower RMSE in 73 settings and higher RMSE in 14. Against trust-region least squares the corresponding outer counts were 76 and 11. Sine gives a reversal: examples-only WSP had lower training loss in nine of nine settings, while the gradient fit had lower outer RMSE in six of nine. Table~\ref{tab:paired_methods} shows both WSP conditions. These are paired counts on the tested configurations. They weight each size and start equally, with six settings for running sum and nine for each other task.

Both WSP conditions reproduced every output on the entire outer set in 30 of 87 settings. Both numerical solvers from the original weights achieved this in zero. On the central or early held-out split the corresponding counts were also 30, 30, zero and zero. Individual exact-match rates can still be substantial in a set with at least one error. The initial Xavier weights achieved zero fully exact outer sets.
    
Examples-only WSP achieved lower outer RMSE than plain full-batch gradient descent in 75 of 87 settings and higher RMSE in 12. The counts were also 75 and 12 against gradient descent followed by L-BFGS. Neither gradient method from its prescribed starting point reproduced an entire outer set exactly. They nevertheless differed on individual tasks. On width-two subtraction, median outer RMSE fell from 0.831 after gradient descent to $1.44\times10^{-13}$ after L-BFGS, with 14 of 2,048 outer examples exactly correct at the latter median. Examples-only WSP had zero RMSE and matched all 2,048 examples at all three starts. Appendix~\ref{app:limitedmemory} gives the full comparisons.
