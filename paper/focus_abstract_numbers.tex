Given examples alone, iteration reduced held-out central or early error in 46 of 87 settings. It lowered extrapolation root mean squared error (RMSE) in \iterBetterE{} settings, tied in \iterTieE{} and raised it in \iterWorseE{} relative to one-shot WSP. Both WSP procedures matched every extrapolation reference in the same \iterPerfectE{} of 87 settings. Full-batch gradient descent using backpropagation achieved this in \perfectGD{} settings, as did descent followed by limited-memory Broyden--Fletcher--Goldfarb--Shanno (L-BFGS) refinement. On two-unit subtraction, median outer RMSE was zero for both WSP procedures, 0.831 for gradient descent and $1.44\times10^{-13}$ after L-BFGS.
